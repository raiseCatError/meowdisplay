# Adaptive Automatic streaming: SIMULATION comparison (issue #123)

**SIMULATION, NOT HARDWARE EVIDENCE.** Every number below comes from the deterministic plant
model in `MacTests/AdaptiveStreamingSimulation.swift`, generated by
`AdaptiveStreamingSimulationTests.testWriteComparisonReport`. The model's capacities, curves and
costs are assumptions chosen to make each scenario legible. They are not measurements of any Mac,
receiver or network, and the table makes no claim about real-world gains.

Regenerate: run this test with `TEST_RUNNER_ADAPTIVE_SIM_REPORT=<path>` passed to `xcodebuild test`.

## What changed since the first table (24 Mbps ladder, campaign 2026-11-17)

- The bitrate ladder now stops at today's Best target, 18 Mbps (`AdaptiveStreamingLadder.bitrateCeiling`).
  The first table let the controller probe up to 24 Mbps, so its healthy rows used more data than the
  fixed policy; they now match it.
- Samples are built by the production builder (`AdaptiveStreamingCoordinator.makeSample`) from what
  production really has: the sender's ping window, send counters and measured encode latency, and the
  receiver `stats` keys the official receivers send, through the wire encoding. `deliveredFps` is
  frames at the presentation hand-off; there is no `dec95` unless a scenario models the Metal path, and
  no `late`, `decodedFps`, `enqFps`, `presAge`, stall count or Window view pane counts. The first table
  fed the controller fields production never sets (encode queue age, decode p95, presented FPS,
  stalls, panes), which hid review finding A1 (no recovery in production).
- Controller changes from review A: missing decode evidence is "unknown" (network headroom plus a
  20 s dwell) instead of "no headroom"; a raster step that can't rise falls through to bitrate; a probe
  with unexplained short delivery reverts; encoder headroom is judged against its 2-interval limit;
  policy clamps count as downward moves for the oscillation guard.
- New scenario: the decoder-limited receiver on the Metal path (with `dec95`), next to the default path.
- Still controller-only: the coordinator's per-receiver restart (20 s) and bitrate (4 s) limiters are
  not modelled here; the wiring tests cover them.

## Model assumptions

- One step per 2 s window, 300 s per scenario, fixed-seed noise (±3 % capacity and RTT).
- Bitrate ladder 2–18 Mbps (top = today's Best); raster 0.5–1.0 of native; FPS from the negotiation.
- Source: distinct frames per second never above its refresh; the stream encodes min(target, source).
- Encoder and decoder: pixel-throughput ceilings (Mpx/s). Above the ceiling, the frame rate falls
  proportionally and queue age / decode time grow. Thermal `critical` throttles the encoder to 60 %.
- Link: capacity C(t); the encoder emits bitrate × motion (motion = encoded fps / 60, min 10 %).
  Demand above C drops frames proportionally, sets send backpressure and inflates RTT; jitter grows
  with utilisation; frames later than one frame interval are not presented (the controller can't see
  that directly: receivers report frames at the presentation hand-off, plus RTT jitter).
- Encode latency (what the sender measures, submit → output): encode time × (1 + utilisation), growing
  steeply above 95 % utilisation.
- Thermal: heat rises with encoder utilisation above an equilibrium (nominal < 0.4 ≤ fair < 0.6 ≤
  serious < 0.8 ≤ critical).
- Fixed policy = today's Automatic: native raster (1.0), 18 Mbps, today's FPS (`StreamingFPSPolicy`);
  it ignores data saver and conditions.

Columns: delivered unique FPS (mean, presented distinct frames); stall s (window × share of
expected unique frames not presented); MB (delivered payload, not billed bytes); mean raster;
s > 60 FPS; final raster/Mbps/FPS; changes; restart-class (raster) changes; direction flips.

## Scenarios

- **Healthy LAN 60 Hz**: iPad 60 Hz, Extend, 300 Mbps, 4 ms RTT.
- **120 Hz source + ProMotion, headroom**: Extend at 120 Hz to a 120 Hz receiver, ample encoder/decoder/link.
- **120 advertised, 60 Hz source**: Mirror of a 60 Hz 1512x982 pt Mac display to a 120 Hz receiver.
- **Bandwidth collapse and recovery**: 300 Mbps, 8 Mbps from 80-180 s, then 300 Mbps.
- **Jittery remote**: Tailscale-style path: 40 Mbps, 45 ms RTT, 40 ms jitter.
- **Metered path (saver)**: iPhone on cellular: 12 Mbps, 60 ms RTT, auto data saver.
- **Encoder-limited Mac**: Encoder ceiling 180 Mpx/s (native 60 needs 240).
- **Decoder-limited old receiver**: Decoder ceiling 150 Mpx/s (native 60 needs 240).
- **Decoder-limited old receiver (Metal path)**: As above, but the receiver reports decode p95 (dec95).
- **Thermal ramp**: Sender heats with encoder load; critical throttles the encoder.
- **Idle to motion**: 1 fps idle to 100 s, 12 fps typing to 200 s, then full motion.
- **Window View multi-pane**: 4 panes + 1 float, 60 fps composition budget, 120 Hz receiver.
- **Route flap**: iPhone Wi-Fi to cellular at 100 s, back at 160 s; stale callbacks at 104 s and 166 s.
- **Telemetry dropout**: No samples 100-140 s while the link drops to 10 Mbps (110-140 s).
- **Two receivers: A (iPad, healthy)**: iPad 60 Hz, Extend, 300 Mbps, 4 ms RTT.
- **Two receivers: B (iPhone, collapse)**: 300 Mbps, 8 Mbps from 80-180 s, then 300 Mbps.

## Reading the table

- The adaptive controller starts at today's quality at 60 FPS and only moves on evidence, so on
  healthy paths the two rows should match (or differ only by confirmed probes).
- Where the plant is constrained, the adaptive row shows the controller's bounded response; the
  size of any difference is a property of this model, not a prediction for real devices.
- Hardware validation is PENDING (see `docs/performance/adaptive-streaming.md`).

## Differences from the first table, row by row (simulation only)

- Healthy rows (healthy LAN, 60 Hz source, idle to motion, Window view, receiver A): the adaptive
  row now equals the fixed one (675 MB, 18 Mbps). The first table's extra data was the 24 Mbps probe.
- 120 Hz ProMotion: still reaches 120 (290 s above 60), now on the evidence production has (no
  `dec95`, capture capped at the current target, saturation as the source signal).
- Decoder-limited receiver: on the default display-layer path the controller can't tell a decoder
  limit from other short delivery, so it holds and matches the fixed policy (37.7 FPS). With `dec95`
  (Metal path) it steps the raster down as before (59.0 FPS). The first table showed the Metal
  behaviour for every receiver.
- Jittery remote: now equals the fixed policy. Receivers report frames at the presentation hand-off,
  so frames presented late (the plant's jitter loss) are invisible to the controller; the first table
  fed it presented FPS directly.
- Bandwidth collapse, route flap, telemetry dropout: similar FPS gains over the fixed policy, with
  less data than the first table (18 Mbps top).
- No hardware claim: every number here is a property of the assumed plant.
